# Diamond High — First E-School Prototype Scope

## Purpose

This document protects the first release from expanding into the entire long-term e-school vision. It defines a focused demonstration of the core learner experience and identifies what should be deferred.

## In scope

### Milestone A — E-school landing page
- Communicate clearly that Diamond High is an online learning platform.
- Explain its educational proposition: understanding, reasoning, application, evidence, and progress.
- Provide a clear route to explore or enter the learning demonstration.
- Establish the initial visual identity and reusable interface foundations.
- Make the layout responsive and accessible.
- Use clearly identified sample content and assets where final content is unavailable.

### Milestone B — One integrated learning journey
- Provide one representative course, subject, or learning path.
- Present at least one structured lesson.
- Include one meaningful practice activity or check for understanding.
- Provide explanatory feedback, not only a bare correct/incorrect result.
- Show a simple and honest progress/completion state.
- Make the next learning step understandable.
- Keep the journey small enough that the creator can explain and maintain it.

### Quality throughout
- Semantic, readable markup and maintainable styles.
- Keyboard-friendly interaction and sensible accessibility practices.
- Responsive behaviour.
- Basic testing and debugging.
- Documentation of significant decisions.
- Version control with clear commits.
- Clear distinction between functional behaviour and static illustrative content.

## Out of scope for the first prototype

Unless evidence makes an item essential, defer:
- A full learning management system.
- Real learner accounts, authentication, role-based permissions, or profile management.
- Persistent storage of real learner records or sensitive information.
- A complete course-authoring system.
- Large assessment banks or complex automated assessment engines.
- Production-grade analytics, predictive scoring, or learner profiling.
- AI tutoring or automated recommendations.
- Payments, subscriptions, and commercial billing.
- Multi-school tenancy, institution administration, or full school integration.
- Claims of production readiness, accreditation, or validated learning outcomes.

## Completion checks

The first prototype is ready for review when:
- The landing page communicates the e-school identity and directs learners toward learning.
- The page remains usable at common desktop and mobile sizes.
- Navigation and visible controls behave as expected.
- A learner can follow the demonstration from course entry through lesson, activity, feedback, and progress without unexplained gaps.
- Feedback gives the learner useful information.
- Progress indicators do not claim more than the demonstration can prove.
- Major limitations and sample-data assumptions are documented.
- The creator can explain the main structure, styling choices, and interactions.
- The prototype can be reviewed without pretending it is a finished platform.

## Change-control rule

When a new idea appears, ask:
1. Does it directly support the current prototype milestone?
2. Is it needed to demonstrate or test a core learning principle?
3. Can it be completed without derailing the milestone?
4. What learner value or learning objective justifies its cost?
5. Does it introduce privacy, safeguarding, or technical obligations beyond the prototype?

If the answer is unclear, record the idea for later instead of expanding the current scope.
